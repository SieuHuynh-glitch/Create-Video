# MASTER VIDEO PRODUCTION PROMPT — NATURAL CYCLE

## 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

Create an experimental cinematic short film titled **“NATURAL CYCLE”**, a surreal, monochromatic visual poem about death, decomposition, rebirth, and the endless cycle of nature.

**Core concept:** Human death can become part of nature’s nutrient cycle, sustaining new life. A skull disintegrates into small fragments that behave symbolically like seeds. These fragments fall into the soil, where a seedling emerges and grows into a tree. The tree eventually produces four skull-shaped fruits resembling the original skull. When the skull fruits fall, the film returns to its opening image, completing an endless visual loop.

The film must communicate its message entirely through imagery, movement, composition, and sound. No dialogue, narration, or explanatory text is needed.

- **Title:** NATURAL CYCLE
- **Genre:** Experimental short film, surrealism, dark art, visual poetry
- **Target duration:** Approximately 2 minutes 30 seconds to 3 minutes 30 seconds, including opening titles and end credits; never exceed 4 minutes
- **Preferred format:** Full HD 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 24 FPS
- **Color palette:** Strict black and white, with grayscale tones permitted only for subtle texture and depth
- **Visual style:** Minimalist, high contrast, organic silhouettes, dark surrealism, fine-art cinematography
- **Sound:** Atmospheric instrumental soundtrack and carefully designed environmental sound effects
- **Narrative structure:** A continuous transformation that ends by returning to its beginning

The result should feel like a carefully composed art film, not a horror trailer, slideshow, or collection of unrelated AI-generated clips.

## 2. REFERENCE IMAGE AND CENTRAL VISUAL MOTIF

Use the user-provided skull artwork as the primary visual reference.

The original image depicts a dense black silhouette resembling a skull or raven-like creature, constructed from feather-like forms, sharp organic contours, dark textures, and large negative spaces against a white background.

Preserve its distinctive silhouette and visual identity throughout the film. The skull shown at the beginning and the skull-shaped fruits appearing later must clearly belong to the same visual world.

The skull is not merely a realistic human skull. It is a surreal black organic sculpture with bird-like and feather-like characteristics. Preserve these features rather than replacing it with a generic anatomical skull.

The user has also provided a new completely black image. Use this newer image as the updated opening reference where appropriate, while retaining the original detailed skull artwork as a reference for the skull’s distinctive organic silhouette and feather-like structure. If the newer image is only a black screen rather than a skull cutout, do not interpret the entire image as the skull; use the intended skull artwork for the actual skull silhouette.

Create a coherent visual system in which:
- The skull represents the end of an individual life.
- Its fragments become symbolic seeds.
- The soil represents transformation and the hidden processes of nature.
- The seedling represents the beginning of new life.
- The mature tree represents growth and continuity.
- Four skull-shaped fruits represent the return of death within the cycle of life.

Maintain a clean white background and strong black silhouettes wherever possible. When the soil appears, use a deep black or very dark gray surface that creates a strong contrast with the white background.

## 3. OPENING TITLE SEQUENCE

**Approximate duration: 0:00–0:10**

Begin with a completely black screen.

Display the film title:

NATURAL CYCLE

Use elegant, restrained typography, preferably a refined serif or minimalist editorial typeface. The title should be centered horizontally and vertically, rendered in white, with generous negative space.

Fade the title in slowly. Keep the typography static and avoid excessive animation, decorative graphics, or additional text.

After the title has been visible long enough to be read, fade or dissolve into the main opening scene.

The opening should establish a quiet, mysterious atmosphere.

## 4. SCENE ONE — THE APPEARANCE OF THE SKULL

**Approximate duration: 0:10–0:25**

Begin with a black screen that gradually transitions to a completely white background.

As the white background becomes visible, reveal the black skull silhouette at the center of the composition.

The skull should emerge gradually from the darkness, as if its form is being revealed rather than suddenly appearing.

Use a fixed, frontal camera. Keep the skull centered and surrounded by generous negative space.

A very subtle push-in may be used before the decomposition begins, but the composition must remain stable.

The skull should appear motionless, heavy, and isolated.

Sound design:
- A low, distant atmospheric drone.
- A faint wind-like texture.
- A restrained, slow pulsing sound that suggests the presence of life without sounding like a literal horror heartbeat.

Avoid jump scares and exaggerated horror effects.

## 5. SCENE TWO — CRACKING AND DISINTEGRATION

**Approximate duration: 0:25–0:50**

The skull begins to decompose.

The camera must remain completely stationary throughout the cracking and fragmentation process.

Do not zoom, pan, tilt, rotate, reframe, or follow individual fragments.

### Stage A: Organic cracks

Small cracks gradually appear across the black skull.

The cracks must have irregular, sharp, organic zigzag trajectories. They should resemble branching fractures rather than square grids, rectangular panels, or clean geometric tiles.

Avoid straight horizontal and vertical divisions.

The cracks should branch unpredictably, with alternating sharp angles and uneven lengths. Their progression should feel organic and physically irregular.

Use thin white fracture lines against the black silhouette, revealing the white background through the cracks.

### Stage B: Separation of fragments

The cracks widen gradually.

Small pieces begin to separate from the skull, followed by larger irregular fragments. The original silhouette becomes increasingly fragmented but remains recognizable during the early part of the sequence.

The fragments must not all detach at once.

Avoid symmetrical explosions, uniform polygonal shattering, or overly geometric debris.

### Stage C: Fragments fall

The skull eventually breaks into many small, irregular, black fragments.

Keep the camera locked in its original position.

The fragments should initially retain their positions relative to the frame, then gradually fall downward under gravity. They should not immediately scatter in all directions or fly toward the camera.

Use subtle differences in falling speed and rotation to create natural movement.

The overall motion should be controlled, slow, and deliberate.

Some fragments may drift slightly sideways, but the dominant movement must be downward.

The fragments symbolically become seeds. They should retain their dark, organic appearance and should not transform into conventional round or brightly colored seeds.

Sound design:
- Fine cracking sounds.
- Subtle dry impacts as fragments separate.
- A restrained layer of falling debris.
- A gradual reduction in the low drone as the skull loses its original shape.

The sound must not overpower the minimalist visual style.

## 6. SCENE THREE — THE SOIL RISES

**Approximate duration: 0:50–1:05**

Maintain the same fixed camera position.

Do not move the camera to follow the fragments.

Instead, introduce the soil from the bottom of the frame. The dark ground surface rises upward from below the image, gradually covering the lower portion of the composition.

The rising soil should visually meet the falling fragments, making it appear that the skull fragments are being buried.

The soil surface should have a slightly irregular organic edge, but remain visually simple and graphic.

Some black fragments disappear beneath the soil surface. Others remain partially visible for a brief moment before being covered.

The soil must stop at a clearly defined height, approximately 70–80% down the frame from the top, leaving enough white space above it for the seedling and the tree to grow.

Once the soil reaches this final height, **its vertical position must remain absolutely fixed for the remainder of the plant-growth sequence**.

Do not animate the soil upward again. Do not change its height, shape, or horizon position. Do not move the camera.

The fixed soil line is essential for visual continuity between the falling fragments, the emergence of the seedling, and the mature tree.

Sound design:
- A soft, granular falling sound.
- Subtle earthy impacts.
- A transition from dry cracking to a softer, muffled atmospheric texture.

## 7. SCENE FOUR — A SEEDLING EMERGES

**Approximate duration: 1:05–1:20**

The soil remains at exactly the same height and in exactly the same position.

The camera remains locked.

Several tiny dark fragments lie partly embedded in the soil. These fragments are symbolic seeds created from the skull’s disintegration.

After a short moment of stillness, one small seed-like fragment begins to produce a tiny sprout.

The sprout emerges slowly from the soil surface.

A delicate stem rises upward. Two small leaves gradually unfold.

The seedling should appear fragile and minimal against the white background.

Other fragments may remain dormant. The scene must focus on one seedling becoming the main subject.

Do not change the ground height or switch to a different camera angle.

The growth should be achieved through smooth, continuous animation rather than an abrupt cut from soil to a fully grown plant.

Sound design:
- Soft, subtle organic textures.
- A faint sense of movement beneath the soil.
- A gentle musical pulse that gradually becomes more noticeable.

## 8. SCENE FIVE — CAMERA SELECTS THE SEEDLING

**Approximate duration: 1:20–1:30**

Once the seedling has emerged, the camera may begin a slow, controlled zoom toward it.

This is the first major camera movement after the locked-off decomposition and germination scenes.

The zoom should be smooth and deliberate, drawing attention to one specific seedling rather than showing all the fragments equally.

The selected seedling becomes the central subject.

Maintain the monochromatic style and keep the soil surface at a consistent visual level within the composition during the transition.

Avoid rapid camera movement, handheld shaking, or sudden changes in perspective.

The purpose of the zoom is to transition from the broad cycle of decomposition to the detailed development of one living organism.

Once the zoom is complete, establish a stable framing for the plant’s growth.

## 9. SCENE SIX — THE TREE GROWS

**Approximate duration: 1:30–1:55**

The seedling gradually develops into a mature, surreal tree.

The growth must be clearly visible and continuous:
1. The stem becomes thicker.
2. The trunk grows upward.
3. Roots extend beneath the soil surface.
4. Branches extend outward and upward.
5. Feather-like leaves appear along the branches.
6. The tree develops a distinctive silhouette.

The tree should not look like a conventional realistic fruit tree. Its form should subtly echo the original skull artwork.

Branches may resemble elongated bones or branching organic structures. Leaves may recall black feathers. The overall silhouette should remain elegant, sparse, and slightly unsettling.

The tree must grow from the same seedling selected in the previous scene.

Keep the visual environment consistent. Do not change the background from white to a colorful forest or introduce unrelated landscapes.

Use time-lapse-inspired animation, but preserve a sense of physical growth. The tree should gradually occupy more of the frame.

The composition should feel balanced and intentional, with sufficient negative space around the branches for the skull-shaped fruits to be revealed.

Sound design:
- A gradual musical swell.
- Soft, subtle creaking as branches extend.
- Light, organic textures that suggest growth.
- A restrained increase in tension as the tree approaches maturity.

## 10. SCENE SEVEN — FOUR SKULL FRUITS APPEAR

**Approximate duration: 1:55–2:15**

When the tree reaches maturity, small dark forms begin to appear on its branches.

These forms gradually develop into skull-shaped fruits.

There must be **exactly four clearly visible skull fruits**.

Do not cover the tree with numerous skulls. The fruits must be sparse, carefully spaced, and visually balanced.

Place the four fruits at different heights and positions across the branches. Avoid arranging them in a straight line or making them perfectly symmetrical.

Each fruit should be attached naturally to a branch by a short stem or organic connection.

The fruits should gradually grow in size and reveal their details.

Their silhouettes must resemble the original black skull artwork, including its distinctive feather-like and raven-like qualities wherever possible.

Do not substitute ordinary white anatomical skulls. The fruits should remain black, stylized, surreal objects that belong to the same visual language as the opening skull.

The transformation should be gradual:
- Small dark buds appear.
- The buds enlarge.
- Their contours become recognizable.
- Feather-like details and the characteristic skull silhouette emerge.

The audience should slowly realize that the tree is producing the same form that was destroyed at the beginning.

The visual effect should be unsettling yet beautiful, expressing the idea that death is not erased but transformed and reintroduced into the cycle.

Keep the camera stable during the fruit-revelation sequence.

Sound design:
- The music reaches its emotional peak.
- Soft branch creaks and subtle organic sounds accompany the fruits’ growth.
- The soundtrack briefly becomes quiet when the skull fruits are fully recognizable.

## 11. SCENE EIGHT — A SKULL FRUIT FALLS

**Approximate duration: 2:15–2:35**

One of the four skull fruits begins to detach from its branch.

The other three skull fruits remain attached to the tree.

Keep the camera at exactly the same angle and framing used for the mature tree and skull fruits.

Do not zoom out, pan, tilt, orbit, or change the camera position when the fruit falls.

The chosen skull fruit detaches naturally and begins to fall downward under gravity.

Its motion should be slow enough for the audience to recognize the skull and observe its descent.

Allow a subtle rotation if needed, but preserve the skull’s recognizable silhouette.

The falling skull moves down through the frame and eventually disappears completely beyond the bottom edge.

**Do not cut away while any part of the falling skull remains visible.**

Wait until the skull has fully exited the frame. Only then transition to the next scene.

The falling fruit represents the completion of one cycle and the beginning of another.

Sound design:
- A subtle snap as the fruit detaches.
- A soft falling sound.
- A gradual reduction in the music.
- A short moment of silence after the fruit disappears.

## 12. SCENE NINE — RETURN TO THE ORIGINAL SKULL

**Approximate duration: 2:35–2:45**

Once the falling skull fruit has completely left the frame, transition back to the original opening composition.

The scene returns to a clean white background with a black skull centered in the frame.

Use the same silhouette, scale, and positioning established in the opening sequence.

The transition should be motivated by the falling skull. For example, the last dark shape may disappear below the frame, followed by a brief dark transition that reveals the original white background and skull.

The original skull must be clearly recognizable as the same form shown at the beginning.

Hold the image long enough for the audience to understand that the cycle has restarted.

Then fade to black.

The visual narrative must form a loop:

SKULL → FRAGMENTS → SOIL → SEEDLING → TREE → FOUR SKULL FRUITS → FALLING SKULL → ORIGINAL SKULL.

The meaning should emerge from the repeated imagery rather than explanatory text.

## 13. AFTER-CREDITS SEQUENCE

**Approximate duration: 2:45–3:05**

After the main story ends, show a minimalist white after-credits board.

The board must be completely blank.

Do not write any title, caption, subtitle, production credit, placeholder, logo, symbol, or decorative text on it.

The board exists so the filmmaker can add their own content later during editing.

Keep the board centered against a simple white or very light background. A thin, subtle outline may be used if it helps distinguish the board from the background, but the board itself must remain empty.

Hold the blank board for approximately 10–15 seconds.

The camera should remain static.

Do not automatically generate credits or text on the board.

## 14. FINAL END CARD

After the blank after-credits board, transition to a completely black screen.

Display only the words:

THE END

Use centered, elegant white typography.

Keep the text simple and restrained, consistent with the opening title.

Hold for approximately 3–5 seconds, then fade to black.

No additional credits, logos, or captions should appear unless added manually by the filmmaker.

## 15. CAMERA AND CONTINUITY RULES

These requirements are essential.

**Locked camera during decomposition:**
The camera must remain completely stationary while the skull cracks, separates, and disintegrates. Individual fragments move; the camera does not.

**Soil movement:**
The soil rises only from the bottom of the frame during the burial transition. Once it reaches its final position, it remains fixed throughout the seedling and tree-growth sequence.

**Seedling focus:**
A controlled zoom toward one selected seedling is permitted after the germination scene. Do not use this zoom to follow individual falling fragments.

**Tree-growth framing:**
Establish a stable composition for the mature tree. Maintain that exact angle and framing during the appearance and falling of the skull fruits.

**Falling fruit:**
Do not change the camera angle while the selected skull fruit falls. Wait until the fruit has completely exited the frame before cutting to the original skull.

**Loop continuity:**
Match the final skull image to the opening skull image in silhouette, scale, position, and background.

Avoid unnecessary camera movements, random reframing, inconsistent perspectives, or changes in the relative height of the ground.

## 16. VISUAL STYLE AND TECHNICAL DIRECTION

Maintain a consistent monochromatic visual identity throughout the film.

Use:
- High-contrast black silhouettes against white negative space.
- Organic, irregular edges.
- Fine, feather-like details.
- Subtle grayscale textures where necessary.
- Smooth and deliberate animation.
- Minimalist compositions.
- Controlled pacing.
- Cinematic fades and motivated transitions.

Avoid:
- Bright colors.
- Cartoon aesthetics.
- Cute or playful skull designs.
- Generic realistic anatomical skulls that do not match the reference.
- Geometric square cracks.
- Symmetrical polygonal explosions.
- Excessive skull fruits.
- Rapid camera movements.
- Unmotivated scene changes.
- Overly graphic depictions of human decomposition.
- Unnecessary text or subtitles.
- Changes to the established soil height during plant growth.

The overall tone should remain contemplative, mysterious, organic, and visually poetic.

## 17. SOUND DESIGN AND MUSIC

Create an original atmospheric instrumental soundtrack without dialogue or narration.

The soundtrack should follow the emotional arc of the film:

**Opening:** Deep ambient drone, subtle wind, restrained low-frequency pulses.

**Decomposition:** Dry cracking sounds, delicate fragmentation, falling debris, and a gradual reduction in the opening drone.

**Burial:** Soft granular impacts and muffled earthy textures.

**Germination:** Quiet organic sounds, subtle pulses, and a gradual emergence of warmth through rhythm rather than color.

**Growth:** A restrained musical swell, soft branch creaks, and delicate natural textures.

**Skull fruits:** A brief rise in tension followed by a moment of stillness when the four skull fruits become recognizable.

**Falling skull:** A subtle detachment sound, falling movement, and a gradual reduction in the soundtrack.

**Loop transition:** A short moment of silence before the original skull reappears.

**After-credits:** Minimal or near-silent ambience.

**The End:** A final sustained tone fading into silence.

Do not use conventional horror stingers, loud jump scares, cheerful orchestral music, or excessive sound effects.

## 18. DETAILED PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

Produce the film in stages to maintain consistency and avoid visual drift.

### Step 1 — Prepare the visual assets

- Import the original skull reference.
- Import the updated black reference image.
- Determine which image is intended as the skull silhouette and which is intended as a black screen.
- Extract the skull silhouette from its background if necessary.
- Preserve the skull’s organic outer contour and feather-like details.
- Prepare a monochrome soil texture.
- Prepare or design irregular fragment shapes.
- Design a consistent seedling, tree, and four skull-fruit assets.

### Step 2 — Create the master composition

- Set the project to 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 24 FPS.
- Establish a pure white background for the opening and principal visual scenes.
- Establish the final soil height and mark its position.
- Define the skull’s opening scale and exact center position.
- Save the composition and reuse these values in later scenes to preserve continuity.

### Step 3 — Animate the skull decomposition

- Begin with the intact skull.
- Draw irregular zigzag cracks that follow the skull’s organic shape.
- Widen the cracks gradually.
- Separate irregular fragments.
- Animate the fragments falling downward while keeping the camera stationary.
- Avoid square tiles and uniform polygonal shards.
- Ensure the fragments retain their black silhouette appearance.

### Step 4 — Animate the soil transition

- Animate the dark soil surface rising from below the frame.
- Let it cover the lower part of the falling fragments.
- Stop the soil at the predefined height.
- Lock the soil position and keep it unchanged during subsequent growth scenes.

### Step 5 — Animate the seedling

- Place small fragments at and below the soil surface.
- Introduce a single seedling.
- Animate the stem emerging and the leaves unfolding.
- Keep the camera fixed during initial germination.
- Gradually zoom toward the selected seedling once it has emerged.

### Step 6 — Grow the tree

- Animate the seedling into a trunk.
- Add roots, branches, and feather-like leaves.
- Preserve the established visual style.
- Maintain a clean silhouette and sufficient negative space.

### Step 7 — Create four skull fruits

- Create exactly four skull-shaped fruits.
- Space them across different branches and heights.
- Keep them sparse rather than crowding the canopy.
- Use the original skull reference for their appearance.
- Animate their growth gradually.
- Keep the camera fixed once the mature tree composition has been established.

### Step 8 — Animate the falling skull

- Select one of the four skull fruits.
- Animate its detachment from the branch.
- Keep the camera locked.
- Let the fruit fall until it completely exits the bottom of the frame.
- Do not begin the transition before the fruit has disappeared.

### Step 9 — Match the opening image

- Return to the original skull silhouette.
- Match its opening size and position.
- Match the white background and overall composition.
- Use a controlled transition to establish the cycle.
- Fade to black after the visual loop has been understood.

### Step 10 — Add titles and credits

- Add the opening title NATURAL CYCLE.
- Add a completely blank after-credits board.
- Add THE END on a black background.
- Do not place any text on the blank board.

### Step 11 — Sound design and final edit

- Compose or generate a coherent atmospheric soundtrack.
- Add subtle, synchronized sound effects.
- Balance sound levels to preserve the quiet visual character.
- Check that the transitions are smooth.
- Verify the total runtime remains under four minutes.

### Step 12 — Quality control and export

Review the entire film from beginning to end.

Check that:
- The title reads NATURAL CYCLE.
- The correct skull reference is used.
- The cracks are irregular and zigzag-shaped, not square.
- The camera remains stationary during skull disintegration.
- The soil rises from the bottom and then stays fixed.
- The seedling grows from the soil without a disruptive cut.
- The tree grows from the selected seedling.
- Exactly four skull fruits appear.
- The fruit-fall shot retains the same camera angle.
- The falling skull completely exits the frame before the transition.
- The ending returns to the original skull.
- The after-credits board is completely blank.
- THE END appears after the blank board.
- No unwanted captions, logos, or watermarks are present.

Export the finished film as an H.264 MP4 with AAC audio, 1920 × 1080 resolution, 24 FPS, and a total runtime below four minutes.

## 19. FINAL CREATIVE INTENTION

The film should not explicitly explain its message. It should allow the viewer to discover it through visual repetition.

At the beginning, the skull appears to be an isolated, lifeless object.

As it fractures, its rigid form loses its integrity.

The fragments fall into the earth and become symbolic seeds.

A single seedling emerges and grows into a tree.

The tree produces four skull-shaped fruits, revealing that the original form has returned in a new context.

One fruit falls, disappears, and gives way to the original skull.

The cycle begins again.

The final emotional impression should be that death is neither a simple ending nor a complete disappearance. It is part of an ongoing process in which matter changes form and life continues through transformation.

**NATURAL CYCLE — nothing truly disappears; it returns in another form.**
